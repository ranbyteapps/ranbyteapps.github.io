What Crop Studio is for

Crop Studio is useful when you want to remove unwanted edges, reframe media for sharing, standardize a batch of files, crop out black bars, or prepare images, GIFs, and videos for different layouts.

  • Crop images with a live preview crop box.
  • Crop animated GIFs and preview GIF motion while the crop box stays visible.
  • Crop videos such as MP4/MOV/M4V where supported.
  • Use aspect presets such as Free, Original, 1:1, 4:3, 16:9, 9:16, and 2.39:1.
  • Enter precise crop values with X, Y, W, and H fields.
  • Use Auto Crop Borders to help remove solid borders or letterboxing.
  • Copy and paste crop boxes between files.
  • Apply the same crop to multiple files.
  • Export cropped copies without overwriting originals.
Local-first note: Crop Studio works on your computer. You do not need to upload private photos, GIFs, or videos to an online crop tool.

How to crop an image, GIF, or video in Grafiq

  1. Open Crop Studio.
    In Grafiq, open More → Crop Studio, or open Crop Studio from the Tools menu if available in your build.
  2. Add files.
    Click + Add Files… and choose the images, GIFs, or videos you want to crop.
  3. Select a file.
    Click a file in the left list to show it in the preview area. For GIFs and videos, the preview starts from a representative frame.
  4. Draw a crop box.
    Click and drag on the preview to draw the crop area.
  5. Fine-tune the crop.
    Drag the crop box, resize it, or enter exact X / Y / W / H values.
  6. Choose an aspect ratio if needed.
    Use Aspect to choose Free, Original, 1:1, 4:3, 16:9, 9:16, or 2.39:1.
  7. Choose an output folder.
    Select where the cropped files should be saved.
  8. Export the crop.
    Click Export Selected for one file, or Export All for every file in the list.

How to crop multiple files the same way

Crop Studio is especially useful when you need consistent framing across a group of files.

  1. Set the crop on one file.
    Draw and adjust the crop box on a representative image, GIF, or video.
  2. Use an aspect preset.
    Choose an aspect ratio such as 16:9, 9:16, or 1:1 if the batch should all use the same shape.
  3. Enable Keep crop centered if useful.
    This helps keep the crop anchored while you adjust size.
  4. Apply the crop to all.
    Use Apply crop to all to apply the same crop box to every file in the list.
  5. Export all files.
    Click Export All to save cropped copies for the whole batch.
Simple rule: For consistent social/video crops, choose an aspect ratio first, enable Keep crop centered, then batch export.

Useful crop presets and options

  • Free: Crop to any shape.
  • Original: Keep the original aspect ratio.
  • 1:1: Square crop for icons, thumbnails, profile images, or marketplaces.
  • 4:3: Traditional photo or presentation-style framing.
  • 16:9: Widescreen crop for YouTube, slides, and landscape video.
  • 9:16: Vertical crop for stories, reels, shorts, or phone-style video.
  • 2.39:1: Cinematic widescreen-style crop.
  • Center Square: Quickly centers a square crop.
  • Center 16:9: Quickly centers a widescreen crop.
  • Auto Crop Borders: Attempts to remove solid borders, letterboxing, black bars, or white margins from the preview frame.

GIF and video cropping notes

  • For GIFs, use Play GIF to preview motion while keeping the crop overlay visible.
  • For videos, enable Force even dimensions when available to improve MP4 export reliability.
  • GIF and video previews may use the first frame or a representative frame for crop setup.
  • Always preview or inspect the exported result, especially for moving subjects.

Keyboard shortcuts

  • Arrow keys: Nudge the crop position by 1 pixel.
  • Shift + Arrow keys: Nudge by 10 pixels.
  • Option/Alt + Arrow keys: Resize the crop box by 1 pixel.
  • Option/Alt + Shift + Arrow keys: Resize by 10 pixels.

Good uses for Crop Studio

  • Crop photos before sharing or uploading.
  • Remove black bars or letterboxing from videos.
  • Make square images for profile pictures, thumbnails, or listings.
  • Crop vertical videos for phone-style layouts.
  • Crop GIFs while checking animation motion.
  • Apply the same crop to a batch of screenshots, photos, or clips.
  • Prepare media for websites, documents, slides, or social posts.

What to watch out for

  • Cropping changes framing; it does not improve quality or add detail.
  • Very tight crops can make an image or video look lower resolution.
  • Auto Crop Borders is a helper, not a guarantee. Complex backgrounds may need manual adjustment.
  • GIF and video crops should be checked after export because motion may reveal framing issues.
  • For videos, odd crop dimensions can cause codec problems, so use Force even dimensions when available.
  • Exports are new cropped files, usually with a cropped filename suffix, so originals stay unchanged.
