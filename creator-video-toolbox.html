Workflow 1: Make a picture-in-picture video

Picture-in-picture is useful for reaction-style videos, tutorials, demonstrations, face-cam overlays, screen recordings, and showing one clip on top of another.

  1. Open Video Tools.
    In Grafiq, open More → Video Tools.
  2. Add the main video.
    Import the primary clip into the timeline.
  3. Choose an overlay video.
    Use the overlay video controls to choose the smaller video that should appear on top.
  4. Set overlay timing.
    Choose when the overlay starts and which part of the overlay video should be used.
  5. Choose size and position.
    Place the overlay in a corner or other available position.
  6. Preview the edit.
    Use Quick Preview to check timing, placement, and audio.
  7. Export the MP4.
    Save the finished video as a new MP4 file.
Intel Mac reminder: On Intel Macs, use a full-opacity rectangle overlay. Circle/bubble overlays and transparency effects are not supported there.

Workflow 3: Crop a video, GIF, or image for a platform

Different platforms and projects need different shapes. Crop Studio can help you crop media into square, widescreen, vertical, or custom framing.

  • 1:1: Square framing for icons, thumbnails, product images, or profile-style media.
  • 16:9: Widescreen video framing.
  • 9:16: Vertical phone-style framing.
  • Free: Custom crop when you do not need a preset shape.
  1. Open Crop Studio.
    Add the image, GIF, or video you want to crop.
  2. Choose an aspect ratio.
    Pick a preset such as 1:1, 16:9, or 9:16.
  3. Draw or adjust the crop box.
    Make sure the important part of the image or video stays inside the crop.
  4. Export the cropped copy.
    Save the cropped media as a new file.

Workflow 4: Make video files smaller

Large videos are harder to send, archive, upload, or test. Downscaler can create smaller copies for sharing or review.

  1. Open Downscaler.
    Choose the video or batch of videos you want to reduce.
  2. Adjust the reduction amount.
    Start with a moderate reduction.
  3. Preview when available.
    Check a short sample before exporting a long video.
  4. Export a smaller copy.
    Save the reduced version and compare it with the original.
Simple rule: Keep the original full-size file. Use downscaled versions for sharing, testing, or quick uploads.

What Grafiq is not trying to replace

Grafiq is not meant to replace a professional video editor for complex timelines, color grading, advanced effects, multicam projects, or large productions. It is meant to be a small utility toolbox for common jobs that creators often need between larger tools.

Best use: Use Grafiq for quick media prep, simple edits, cleanup, conversion, cropping, resizing, and lightweight creator tasks. Use a full video editor when you need advanced production features.

What to watch out for

  • Video export re-encodes files, so quality, file size, and processing time can change.
  • Large videos can take time to preview or export.
  • Always preview before doing a long export.
  • On Intel Macs, Video Tools overlays are opaque rectangle only.
  • GIFs can be large and color-limited; MP4 is often better for longer motion.
  • Downscaling makes files smaller but can also soften detail.
  • Keep original media files until you confirm the exported copies look right.
