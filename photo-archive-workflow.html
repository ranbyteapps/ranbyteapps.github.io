The basic family photo workflow

A good archive workflow does not need to be complicated. The main idea is to scan, split, name, organize, describe, and back up your photos.

  1. Scan the photos. Use your scanner or phone scanning workflow to create image files or scanned pages.
  2. Split scanned sheets. Use Photo Splitter when one scan contains multiple printed photos.
  3. Rename files clearly. Use Rename Files to create stable, readable filenames.
  4. Organize into folders. Use Organizer to copy or move files by type, date, or project folder.
  5. Find duplicates. Use Duplicate Finder before your archive gets too messy.
  6. Add descriptions or captions. Use metadata tools, CSV workflows, or visible Caption & Overlay depending on your goal.
  7. Create a slideshow if desired. Use Cinematic Slideshow to turn selected photos into a video-style memory project.
Local-first note: Family photos can be personal. Grafiq is designed for local desktop workflows, so you do not need to upload old photos or family archives to an online editing service just to split, rename, caption, or organize them.

Step 1: Scan your photos

Start with the best scans you can reasonably make. Better source files make every later step easier.

  • Clean the scanner glass before scanning.
  • Leave a little space between printed photos when scanning multiple photos on one page.
  • Use a plain background when possible.
  • Save a high-quality original scan before making edited copies.
  • Keep raw scans in a separate folder so you can return to them later.
Simple rule: Keep one folder for original scans and another folder for edited or split photos. Do not treat edited copies as your only archive.

Step 2: Split scanned photo sheets

If one scan contains several printed photos, use Grafiq Photo Splitter to turn the page into separate image files.

  1. Open Photo Splitter.
    In Grafiq, open More → Photo Splitter.
  2. Open the scanned page.
    Load the scan that contains multiple photos.
  3. Use Auto-Detect.
    Try automatic detection first. Use plain-background mode for scanner-bed style images, or album/textured mode for more difficult backgrounds.
  4. Adjust crop boxes manually.
    Move, resize, add, or delete boxes until each photo has the correct crop.
  5. Export separate photos.
    Save each detected photo as its own JPG or PNG file.

Mac note: Photo Splitter can open scanned PDFs directly on Mac. On Windows, convert scanned PDF pages to JPG first using Grafiq Converter, then open the JPG in Photo Splitter.

Step 3: Rename files into a stable sequence

Scanners often create names like Scan001, IMG_4521, or Untitled. Renaming files early makes the archive easier to manage.

  1. Open Rename Files.
    Use More → Rename Files.
  2. Select the split photo files.
    Choose the files from your exported photo folder.
  3. Use sequential naming.
    Choose a base name such as family-archive, mom-album, grandparents, or summer-1984.
  4. Use padding.
    Use numbering like 001, 002, and 003 so files sort correctly.
  5. Review before large batches.
    Test on a few files first if you are not sure.
Good naming example: family-archive-001.jpg, family-archive-002.jpg, family-archive-003.jpg

Step 4: Organize photos into folders

Once the photos are split and named, use Organizer to copy or move them into cleaner folders.

  • Organize by project, album, person, decade, year, or event.
  • Use Copy first when testing a new organization plan.
  • Use Move only when you are sure the preview is correct.
  • Create destination folders such as Family Archive, Scanned Photos, Mom Album, or Vacation 1984.
  • Keep original scans separate from finished photo exports.

Step 5: Find duplicate images

Duplicate photos happen quickly during scanning, exporting, editing, and backing up. Grafiq Duplicate Finder can help you find exact or visually similar images.

  1. Open Find Image Duplicates.
    Choose the folder you want to inspect.
  2. Choose a detection method.
    Use hash matching for exact duplicates, or pixel similarity for visually similar images.
  3. Review duplicate groups.
    Preview before deleting or moving anything.
  4. Move duplicates before deleting.
    For safety, move suspected duplicates into a review folder first.
Archive safety tip: Do not delete old family photos too quickly. Move duplicates to a review folder first, then decide later after checking quality and context.

Step 6: Add captions and descriptions

Family photos become much more valuable when names, dates, places, and notes are attached to them. Grafiq gives you more than one way to do that.

  • Metadata captions: Store descriptions inside supported image files without changing how the image looks.
  • CSV metadata workflow: Review and prepare captions in a spreadsheet-style file, then import descriptions where supported.
  • Visible Caption & Overlay: Draw names, dates, or notes directly onto exported image copies.
Simple rule: Use metadata captions for archive information. Use visible captions when you want the text to appear directly on the image.

Suggested caption fields for family photos

  • Who is in the photo?
  • Where was it taken?
  • When was it taken?
  • What event was happening?
  • Who provided or identified the photo?
  • Was it scanned from an album, loose print, slide, or document?

Step 7: Create a slideshow video

After organizing and captioning a set of photos, you may want to turn them into something easier to watch and share. Grafiq Cinematic Slideshow can turn selected images into a video-style slideshow.

  • Create a slideshow from a folder of family photos.
  • Arrange photos in story order.
  • Add simple timing and motion.
  • Use captions or title cards where supported.
  • Export a video copy while keeping the original photos unchanged.

Good folder structure for a family photo archive

There is no single perfect folder structure, but simple is better than clever.

  • Original Scans — untouched scanner output.
  • Split Photos — individual photos exported from scanned sheets.
  • Edited Copies — cropped, captioned, cleaned, or adjusted versions.
  • Duplicates Review — possible duplicates to check before deleting.
  • Slideshow Projects — finished slideshow videos or project files.

Good uses for this workflow

  • Scanning old family albums.
  • Splitting multiple printed photos from one scan.
  • Cleaning up inherited photo folders.
  • Adding names and dates to old pictures.
  • Preparing family photos for relatives.
  • Creating memorial, anniversary, reunion, or birthday slideshows.
  • Building a long-term personal archive without uploading everything to a website.

What to watch out for

  • Keep original scans before editing or splitting.
  • Do not delete duplicates until you check resolution, cropping, and image quality.
  • Visible captions become part of the exported image.
  • Metadata captions may not be visible in every app.
  • CSV imports depend on filenames or paths matching correctly.
  • Back up important photo folders before doing large rename, move, or cleanup jobs.
