What Cinematic Slideshow is for

Cinematic Slideshow is useful when you want to turn multiple still images into a video instead of sharing a folder of separate photos. It can help you build a simple story from images by adding motion, timing, transitions, and optional text.

  • Turn a group of photos into a slideshow-style video.
  • Arrange images in the order you want.
  • Set timing so each image stays on screen long enough.
  • Add motion such as gentle zoom or pan effects where available.
  • Use transitions between images.
  • Add captions or text where supported.
  • Preview before exporting when available.
  • Export a finished video file without changing the original images.
Local-first note: Cinematic Slideshow works on your computer. You do not need to upload private family photos or project images to an online slideshow maker.

How to create a slideshow video in Grafiq

  1. Open Cinematic Slideshow.
    In Grafiq, open More → Cinematic Slideshow.
  2. Add images.
    Add the photos, scans, screenshots, or graphics you want to include in the slideshow.
  3. Put images in order.
    Arrange the images in the sequence you want viewers to see them.
  4. Set slide timing.
    Choose how long each image should stay on screen.
  5. Choose motion settings.
    Use subtle zoom, pan, or cinematic movement settings where available.
  6. Choose transitions.
    Pick transition behavior between slides, such as simple fades or other available transition options.
  7. Add captions if needed.
    Add text for dates, places, names, project notes, or short story captions where supported.
  8. Preview the slideshow.
    Check pacing, order, motion, and text before exporting.
  9. Export the video.
    Save the finished slideshow as a new video file.
Simple rule: Keep motion subtle and give each photo enough time on screen. A calm slideshow usually feels more polished than one with fast movement on every image.

Good photos for a slideshow

  • Family photos from an event, trip, or holiday.
  • Scanned old photos from an archive project.
  • Product images that need a simple video presentation.
  • Before-and-after project photos.
  • Graduation, birthday, wedding, memorial, or celebration images.
  • Landscape or travel photos that look good with slow pan or zoom.
  • Images that are large enough to handle motion without becoming too soft.

How to make the slideshow feel better

  • Start strong: Put one of the best images near the beginning.
  • Group related photos: Keep similar scenes, people, or events together.
  • Use simple timing: Longer timing works better for emotional or detail-heavy photos.
  • Use subtle motion: Slow zooms and pans often look cleaner than extreme movement.
  • Use captions sparingly: Short captions are easier to read than long blocks of text.
  • Preview the whole thing: A slideshow can feel different when watched from beginning to end.

Caption ideas

Captions can help viewers understand the story without turning the slideshow into a document. Short text usually works best.

  • Names of people in older family photos.
  • Dates or approximate years.
  • Locations, trips, or events.
  • Short phrases such as “Summer 1984” or “First house.”
  • Project stages such as “Before,” “During,” and “After.”
  • Product names or simple callouts.

Cinematic Slideshow vs. Motion Effects

  • Cinematic Slideshow: Best when you want to turn multiple images into a finished sequence or story.
  • Motion Effects: Best when you want to create a motion clip from one still image.
Good workflow: Use Motion Effects for one image when you want a single animated clip. Use Cinematic Slideshow when you want a full photo sequence exported as a video.

Good uses for Cinematic Slideshow

  • Create a family photo video.
  • Turn scanned photos into an archive slideshow.
  • Make a simple memorial or celebration video.
  • Create a before-and-after project video.
  • Make a product image slideshow.
  • Create simple video inserts for other editing projects.
  • Turn a folder of photos into something easier to watch and share.

What to watch out for

  • Low-resolution images may look soft if zoomed too far.
  • Too many fast transitions can make the slideshow feel busy.
  • Long captions can be hard to read before the next slide appears.
  • Portrait and landscape images may need different framing choices.
  • Video export can take time for large projects.
  • Always review the exported video before sharing it.
  • Keep your original images unchanged until you confirm the final video looks right.
